When the last god has fallen, Surtr is still standing. He hurls fire across the world. Flames lick the sky itself. The stars fall, and the earth sinks beneath the sea.

That's the end of the world, but not the end of the story.

The seeress looks once more and sees the earth rising from the sea, green and fresh, with fields growing crops no one planted. Waterfalls tumble from the new hills, and an eagle flies over them, hunting fish.

A few gods survive: Vidar and Vali, sons of Odin, and Modi and Magni, sons of Thor, who inherit Mjollnir. They meet on the old field of Idavellir and find the golden game pieces the gods once played with. Then, Baldur and Hodur return from the dead, reconciled.

The sun had a daughter before the wolf swallowed her, just as bright, and she takes her mother's place. Two humans, Lif and Lifthrasir, hid through the fire and now repopulate the world. They survived by hiding in a wood called Hoddmimir's Holt, living on the morning dew.

And the seeress glimpses a hall roofed in gold called Gimle, where the righteous live. In that hall, the seeress says, there is joy forever.

Scholars suspect Christian influence here.

Not everything is cleansed, though. The dragon Nidhogg still flies by, carrying corpses on his wings. Then the seeress sinks back down, and the vision ends.
