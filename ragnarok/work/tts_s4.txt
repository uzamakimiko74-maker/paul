Two wolves have chased the sun and moon since the beginning. Now they catch them. The sky goes dark, and the stars vanish. Their names are Sköll and Hati. Sköll takes the sun, and Hati takes the moon.

The earth shakes so hard that mountains fall, and every chain in the world snaps. That frees Loki, and it frees Fenrir, the monstrous wolf the gods once tricked and bound.

Fenrir charges with his lower jaw scraping the ground and his upper jaw touching the sky. Flames burn from his eyes and nostrils. Beside him comes his brother, Jormungandr, the world serpent, who has circled the earth in the ocean. He heaves out of the sea, flooding the land and spitting venom across the sky.

The ship Naglfar, built from the nails of the dead, breaks loose on the flood, packed with giants, with Loki at the helm. From the south ride the sons of Muspel, led by the fire giant Surtr, whose sword outshines the sun. They cross Bifrost, the rainbow bridge, and it breaks beneath them. Bifrost is the only road between heaven and earth. And now it is gone.

Heimdall, watchman of the gods, blows Gjallarhorn, and all nine worlds hear it. Odin rides to the well of Mimir for counsel. That well is where Odin once gave up an eye for wisdom. Now he asks it for advice one last time.

The world tree Yggdrasil shakes. In Valhalla, Odin's hall, there are five hundred and forty doors, and eight hundred warriors can march through each one side by side.

Then the gods arm themselves, along with the warriors of Valhalla, chosen to die in battle and wait for exactly this day, and ride out to meet the end.
