In almost every other mythology, the gods can't lose. In the Norse world, they can, and they know it. Fate is stronger than Odin. Odin spent his whole life collecting knowledge to escape this fate. He hung from a tree, gave up an eye, and still could not change it.

The whole point is what they do with that knowledge. They don't hide or bargain. They prepare and they ride out. For the Vikings, courage meant standing your ground when you know how it ends. There is a famous line in the Havamal: cattle die, kinsmen die, you yourself will die, but fame never dies for the one who earns a good name.

And the world burns and the world returns. Nothing is permanent, not even the end. Some scholars think the rebirth was shaped by Christian ideas, and some think it is much older. Nobody can prove it.
