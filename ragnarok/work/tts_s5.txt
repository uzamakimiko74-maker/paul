The armies meet on a plain called Vigridr, a hundred leagues across. It plays out as a series of duels, each god against the enemy they were always meant to face. The odds are not even. Gods who can die against monsters who cannot be reasoned with, and every one of them knows exactly how it ends.

Odin versus Fenrir. Odin charges in a golden helmet with his spear Gungnir. Fenrir swallows him whole. The father of the gods is gone, exactly as the prophecy said. But Odin’s silent son Vidar steps forward, wearing a thick shoe made from every scrap of leather ever trimmed from shoes. He plants it on the wolf’s lower jaw, grips the upper, and tears Fenrir apart. In another version, he stabs it through the heart.

Thor versus Jormungandr. Thor meets the serpent he once fished for and nearly killed. Their last meeting was out at sea, when Thor used an ox head as bait and hauled the whole serpent up to his boat. This time, Mjölnir finishes it. Thor takes nine steps away. Then the venom catches up, and he falls dead.

Freyr versus Surtr. Freyr faces the lord of fire without his sword because he gave it away long ago to win the giantess Gerd. He has only an antler. That sword could fight by itself, and without it, even a god can run out of luck. Surtr kills him.

Týr versus Garm. Týr, who lost his hand binding Fenrir, fights the hound of Hel. They kill each other.

Heimdall versus Loki. Old enemies face to face at last. Both die. Heimdall, who sees for a hundred miles and hears the grass grow, never saw this one coming.

Odin, Thor, Freyr, Týr, Heimdall. Gone in moments. The gods who held the world together are gone, and what is left will burn.

The prophecy was exact.
