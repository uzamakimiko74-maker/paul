Almost everything comes from two Icelandic books from the 1200s: the Poetic Edda, especially a poem called Völuspá, where Odin raises a dead seeress and demands to know the future, and the Prose Edda by Snorri Sturluson, who retells it as a conversation. The oldest copy of the Poetic Edda is a calfskin manuscript called the Codex Regius, written around twelve seventy. In Völuspá, the seeress tells Odin what she sees coming, and she keeps asking him one question, like a drumbeat: do you understand yet? Snorri was no monk. He was a powerful Icelandic chieftain and poet, and he wrote his Edda as a handbook for poets who wanted to understand the old stories.

One warning: both were written down by Christians centuries after the old religion faded, so historians debate how much of the ending is purely Norse. I'll flag where the sources disagree. The word Ragnarök itself means the fate of the gods, though some manuscripts say the twilight of the gods, and that small difference tells you how messy these sources are.
