The death of Baldur. Ragnarök does not begin with a battle. It begins with a funeral.

It starts with Baldur, Odin's radiant, beloved son. After dreaming of his own death, his mother, Frigg, makes everything in the world swear never to harm him. Fire swears, and water, and iron, and every metal. Stones swear, and trees, and diseases, and every beast, bird, and snake.

The gods turn it into a game, throwing weapons and watching them bounce off.

But Loki learns Frigg skipped one plant, mistletoe, too young to bother with. He makes a dart from it, hands it to Baldur's blind brother, Höðr, and guides his aim. Baldur drops dead. And they cannot take revenge, because the place is sacred ground.

The goddess Hel will release him only if every living thing weeps. Everything does, except one giantess in a cave. Baldur stays dead, and many say that giantess was Loki in disguise. Odin understands what this means. In the seeress's vision, Baldur's death is the first crack in the world.

Loki's punishment.

The gods bind Loki in a cave with the entrails of his own son and hang a venomous snake above his face. His wife, Sigyn, catches the venom in a bowl, but when she has to empty it, the poison hits him and he thrashes. The Norse said that was what caused earthquakes. When Loki breaks free, it's over. To understand why, you need to know Loki's children. Loki fathered three monsters with a giantess: the wolf Fenrir, the world serpent Jormungandr, and Hel, who is half living and half dead. Odin hurled the serpent into the sea, where it grew until it circled the whole earth and bit its own tail. And the gods raised Fenrir themselves, until he grew so huge that they were afraid. So they asked the dwarves for a ribbon called Gleipnir, made from the sound of a cat's footsteps, the beard of a woman, the roots of a mountain, and the breath of a fish. Fenrir smelled a trick. He agreed to be tied only if one god put a hand in his mouth as a pledge. Only Týr dared. The ribbon held. Fenrir bit down. And Týr lost his right hand, but the wolf stayed bound until the end of the world.

Fimbulwinter.

The countdown is a winter, not one, but three in a row with no summer between. Snow blows in from every side, and the sun gives no warmth. Human society collapses. Brothers kill brothers. Oaths are broken. The seeress describes the world before the end like this: an age of axes, an age of swords, shields split in two, an age of wind, an age of wolves, before the world crashes down. Everything that holds a society together is cut away, one thing at a time.

Then the alarm sounds. Three roosters crow, one among the giants, one among the gods, one in the halls of the dead, and in the cave of Gnipahellir, the hound Garm howls.

It has begun.
